# Democracy — English source script

Almost every country in the world calls itself a democracy. Even North Korea. Its official name is the Democratic People's Republic of Korea. So what does the word actually mean?

It comes from Greek. Demos means people. Kratos means power. Power of the people.

It started in Athens, about two and a half thousand years ago. Around 508 BC, a leader named Cleisthenes gave ordinary citizens the right to decide. Thousands of them gathered on a hill called the Pnyx, listened to speeches, and voted by raising their hands. If one man became too powerful, citizens could scratch his name on a piece of pottery. With enough votes, he was sent out of the city for ten years.

Sounds fair. But here is the catch. Women could not vote. Slaves could not vote. Foreigners could not vote. In the end, only a small share of the people living in Athens had a voice.

Today most democracies work differently. There are too many of us to gather on one hill, so we choose representatives. But a real democracy needs more than elections. It needs a real choice between candidates, independent courts, a free press, and the right to criticize the government without fear.

That is the difference. Many countries hold elections. Far fewer have all of these.

So next time you hear the word democracy, ask one simple question: can the people actually change who is in power?
