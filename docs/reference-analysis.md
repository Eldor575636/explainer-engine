# Reference analysis

## 1. Bir Foiz (@birfoiz_) — "Marko Rubio" (Uzbek, 86 s, 720x1280)

Topic: who Marco Rubio is (US Secretary of State) on his visit to Samarkand, explained for Uzbek viewers.

**How it's made**
- AI-illustrated stills in a muted vintage-poster / risograph style with paper texture.
- **Layered**: background plates (Samarkand, State Department, White House, Capitol) with the
  character as a separate **cut-out sticker with a white outline** placed on top.
- **Motion**: the character pops/scales in; a plane flies across the sky; labels pop in one after
  another (title → mustard/teal tag → small italic subtitle); stamps slam down ("YO'Q!", "KAMYOB!");
  hand-drawn arrows draw themselves ("maslahat" → President); words get struck through and replaced
  ("KELIN" → "KELISHUV ✓"); list items build up line by line; soft dissolves between scenes.
- Continuous background music under the voice, plus sound effects.
- Keyword labels on screen rather than full word-by-word captions. Logo "1% BIR FOIZ" in a corner,
  Instagram handle on some frames.

**Why it works**
- Local metaphors: "Dunyo = katta mahalla", Rubio as the *sovchi* (matchmaker), the polyclinic
  queue number for presidential succession ("4 · Davlat kotibi").
- Something new on screen every 1–3 seconds.
- Ends with a direct question to the audience ("Siz-chi? Amerika vizasi / Green karta —
  izohda rostini yozing!") to drive comments.

**What we take vs avoid**
- Take: layering, pop-in stickers, sequenced labels, stamps, drawn arrows, local metaphors,
  pace, music bed, comment-driving ending.
- Avoid: copying their look. Ours is bold flat navy/red/mustard on cream, not muted vintage.
  Their near-realistic AI portraits of living politicians are a risk we avoid.
